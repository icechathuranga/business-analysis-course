Cheat sheet — prioritisation methods compared

BABOK's umbrella technique is simply Prioritization [BABOK]. The five methods below are widely-used industry ways of putting it into practice — different lenses on the same task. Whichever you use, prioritise against a fixed boundary (a deadline or budget), or the exercise is meaningless. (See Module 8.)

MethodHow it worksBest whenLimitation
MoSCoW [industry]Sort requirements into Must, Should, Could and Won't-have-this-time, against a fixed deadline or budgetA fast, non-numeric first pass that a mixed business audience grasps easilyEverything inflates to "Must" unless the Musts are challenged hard
Value vs effort [industry]Plot each item by business value against effort or cost on a 2×2; do quick wins firstSequencing within a MoSCoW band, or showing why a cheap-but-pointless item loses to a modest quick winHides dependencies, and treats effort and cost as if they were the same
Kano model [industry]Classify features as basic, performance or delighter by how they affect satisfaction"Value" is too blunt and you need to explain why a feature matters (for the business case, or marketing)No delighter compensates for a broken basic; delighters decay into basics over time
WSJF [industry]Weighted Shortest Job First: rank by cost of delay (value plus time criticality plus risk or opportunity) divided by job sizeA larger backlog where team capacity varies and you want a defensible, repeatable order (agile)Invites false precision; scores are relative, so re-score the whole set together, never in isolation
100-dollar / weighted scoring [industry]Each stakeholder spends 100 points across the items (or scores them against weighted criteria); total to rankCompeting stakeholders need buy-in to the process, not just the outcomeRewards coalitions and can be dominated by one senior voice unless every vote counts equally

How to choose

Combine lenses deliberately: MoSCoW for the business conversation, value vs effort to sequence within a band, WSJF when capacity varies, Kano to explain, 100-dollar to build buy-in. Using one method for everything is the beginner's tell. Prioritisation is a negotiation, not a calculation — the methods structure the conversation and make trade-offs visible, but their output is an input to a decision, never the decision itself. At Homegrove the three Musts (real-time stock, automatic order import, delivery-status visibility) are the root cause plus the symptom customers feel — not the website rebuild.
