Cheat sheet — which diagram or model when

Pick the model that answers your question, not your favourite notation. Process models show how work flows; behaviour models show how people and systems interact; data models show what the work creates and uses. (See Modules 2 and 6.)

ModelShowsUse when
Flowchart [industry]Steps (boxes), decisions (diamonds) and flow for a single processSketching one straightforward process or decision path quickly
BPMN [industry]A precise, standard process model (BPMN — Business Process Model and Notation) with events, tasks, gateways and message flows between pools; an Object Management Group (OMG) notation used within Process Modelling [BABOK]A process must be precise, shared with technical people, or feed automation
Swimlane / cross-functional map [industry]A process split into lanes per role or system, so hand-offs appear as arrows crossing lanesA process crosses people, teams or systems and the problem may be in a hand-off
SIPOC [industry]Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs and Customers on one pageAgreeing the scope and boundaries of a process in minutes, before detailed mapping
Value stream map [industry]The end-to-end flow, separating value-adding time from wait timeSpeed or delay is the complaint and you need to see where the time goes
Use Cases and Scenarios [BABOK]How an actor uses the solution to reach a goal, via a main success scenario and extensionsYou need the detailed interaction between a user and a system
User journey map [industry]The customer's experience across a journey — actions, emotions and pain pointsImproving experience end to end and finding the low point to fix
Data Flow Diagrams (DFD) [BABOK]How data moves between external entities, processes and data storesYou want to spot where information is duplicated, re-entered or lost
Entity relationship diagram (ERD)Entities, their attributes and the relationships, with cardinality, between them — from the Data Modelling [BABOK] techniqueThe solution stores and relates information and everyone must mean the same by "order" or "stock"
State diagramThe states a thing moves through and the allowed transitions — from the State Modelling [BABOK] techniqueA key entity, such as an order, has a meaningful life cycle
Context diagram [industry]The system in the middle and the external entities it exchanges with (a lightweight Scope Modelling [BABOK])Setting scope — agreeing what is inside the system and what is outside it
Decision table / DMN [industry]Every combination of conditions and the outcome for each; puts Decision Modelling [BABOK] into practice, and DMN (Decision Model and Notation) is an OMG standardA decision has several conditions and prose would hide a missing case

How to choose

A swimlane answers "who does what, and where are the hand-offs?"; a value stream, "where does the time go?"; a DFD, "where does information duplicate?"; an ERD, "what do we store and how does it relate?"; a context diagram, "what is in scope?". Keep each diagram at one level of detail, model the as-is honestly before you design the to-be, and always follow a model with the "so what?" — a diagram with no analysis changes nothing. At Homegrove one swimlane of the order-to-fulfilment process exposes the twice-daily re-keying as the root cause.
