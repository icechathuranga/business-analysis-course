Resources & further reading

A short, deliberately conservative list of resources that are genuinely well known in the profession. Treat it as a starting point and always confirm the current edition or details at source.

The standard and the body behind it

  • The IIBA (International Institute of Business Analysis) — the main professional body for business

analysis, which publishes the body of knowledge and the certifications. Its official website is the place to confirm anything about certification.

  • The BABOK® Guide, version 3 — A Guide to the Business Analysis Body of Knowledge, published by

the IIBA. Widely regarded as the standard reference for the profession and the basis for the exams. It is the authority this course maps to; treat it as the single source of truth for technique names.

Certifications (the IIBA ladder)

A rough progression by experience; check the IIBA for current eligibility, fees, exam format and renewal rules, as these change.

  • ECBA™ — Entry Certificate in Business Analysis: new to the field.
  • CCBA® — Certification of Capability in Business Analysis: a few years' experience.
  • CBAP® — Certified Business Analysis Professional: senior / expert.
  • AAC — Agile Analysis Certification: a specialist agile credential alongside the core ladder.

Books widely recommended by BAs

Titles and lead authors only — always get the current edition. If a book is not listed here, that does not mean it is not good.

  • The BABOK® Guide (IIBA) — the official guide, as above.
  • Business Analysis, published by BCS, the Chartered Institute for IT — a well-known general

textbook, especially for UK and BCS study routes.

  • Software Requirements by Karl Wiegers — a widely recommended classic on eliciting, writing and

managing requirements.

  • User Stories Applied by Mike Cohn — a standard introduction to user stories in agile delivery.
  • User Story Mapping by Jeff Patton — the standard reference for story mapping.
  • Writing Effective Use Cases by Alistair Cockburn — a long-standing reference on use cases.

The IIBA, BCS and many publishers also produce technique handbooks and exam-study guides; prefer the most recent edition.

Free tools a BA can use

You can do most BA work with free, general tools:

  • A diagramming tool — draw.io / diagrams.net is free and covers flowcharts, swimlanes, BPMN, ERDs,

context diagrams and more.

  • A spreadsheet — for traceability matrices, prioritisation grids, RACI charts and the business-case

numbers.

  • A notes or documentation tool — for requirements, meeting notes, a project glossary and the

stakeholder register.

Communities

  • The IIBA and its local chapters — events, study groups and a route to meet other practitioners.
  • BA discussion forums and subreddits — useful for real-world, day-to-day questions; weigh the

advice you find against the official guide, since opinions vary.

  • A workplace community of practice — often the fastest way to learn how BA work is really done in

your own organisation.
