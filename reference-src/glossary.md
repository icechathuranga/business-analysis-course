# Glossary of BA terms

Plain-English definitions of the terms used across the course. Named techniques are tagged
**[BABOK]** (one of the 50 named BABOK® techniques) or **[industry]** (a widely-used method the
course also teaches); general concepts carry no tag. BABOK proper names are spelled as the guide
spells them.

- **Acceptance criteria** — the conditions a requirement must meet to be accepted as done, often written in the Given/When/Then (Gherkin) form. They become the test conditions at UAT. Relate to Acceptance and Evaluation Criteria [BABOK].
- **Adaptive** — BABOK's term for an agile, iterative way of working, where requirements emerge and are reprioritised each cycle rather than fixed up front (contrast predictive).
- **As-is** — how a process or system works today, before any change. You model it honestly before designing the to-be.
- **BABOK** — A Guide to the Business Analysis Body of Knowledge, the IIBA's standard reference, organised into six knowledge areas plus underlying competencies, perspectives and about 50 techniques.
- **Backlog** — an ordered, living list of requirements or user stories still to be delivered, kept prioritised and refined. Managed through Backlog Management [BABOK].
- **Baseline** — an agreed, signed-off snapshot of requirements (or a metric) that change is measured against. Anything proposed after it goes through change control.
- **BPMN** — Business Process Model and Notation: a standard process-diagram notation from the Object Management Group (OMG), with events, tasks, gateways and message flows. An OMG notation used within Process Modelling [BABOK]. [industry]
- **BRD** — Business Requirements Document: a written statement of the business and stakeholder requirements, common on predictive projects. [industry]
- **Business analysis** — the practice of identifying business needs and recommending solutions that deliver value; its highest-value work is framing the problem before a solution is chosen.
- **Business case** — a structured justification for a proposed change, weighing costs, benefits, risks and options to support a funding decision. Relates to Business Cases [BABOK].
- **Business requirement** — a high-level need or outcome of the organisation — the *why*. Homegrove: cut the late-delivery rate from 12% to under 4%.
- **Business rule** — a policy or constraint that governs a decision or behaviour, stated independently of any system so it can be changed on its own. From Business Rules Analysis [BABOK].
- **CATWOE** — a Soft Systems checklist — Customers, Actors, Transformation, Worldview, Owner, Environment — for examining a messy situation whole. [industry]
- **Change request** — a proposal to alter a baselined requirement or scope. It is assessed for benefit, cost, risk and impact before anyone accepts it.
- **Context diagram** — a one-page view of a system in the middle and the external entities it exchanges with, used to set scope. A lightweight Scope Modelling [BABOK]. [industry]
- **Decision table** — a grid laying out every combination of conditions and the outcome for each, so no case is missed. Puts Decision Modelling [BABOK] into practice; the core of DMN (Decision Model and Notation). [industry]
- **DFD** — Data Flow Diagram: shows how information moves between external entities, processes and data stores — not the time order. From Data Flow Diagrams [BABOK].
- **Elicitation** — drawing needs out of stakeholders rather than merely collecting them, because people give you symptoms and solutions, not clean requirements.
- **Epic** — a user story too large to deliver or test in one go, later broken into smaller stories (see functional decomposition).
- **ERD** — Entity Relationship Diagram: shows the things a business cares about (entities), their attributes and the relationships between them, with cardinality. From Data Modelling [BABOK].
- **Functional requirement** — a statement of what the solution must *do* — a behaviour or feature. A type of solution requirement (contrast non-functional).
- **Gap analysis** — comparing the current state with the desired future state; each difference becomes something to change or build. [industry]
- **Gherkin** — the Given/When/Then format for writing testable acceptance criteria. [industry]
- **IIBA** — the International Institute of Business Analysis, the professional body that publishes the BABOK Guide and the BA certifications.
- **INVEST** — a quality checklist for user stories: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable. [industry]
- **Kano model** — a way to sort features by their effect on satisfaction: basic (must-be), performance, and delighter. [industry]
- **Knowledge area** — one of the six groupings of related BABOK tasks, such as Elicitation and Collaboration. The areas interleave; they are not a sequence.
- **KPI** — Key Performance Indicator: a measure, with a baseline and a target, that shows whether an objective is being met. From Metrics and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) [BABOK].
- **MoSCoW** — a prioritisation method sorting requirements into Must, Should, Could and Won't-have-this-time, against a fixed deadline or budget. [industry]
- **MVP** — Minimum Viable Product: the smallest release that delivers real value and lets a team learn from real use. A common agile idea. [industry]
- **NFR** — Non-Functional Requirement: a quality the solution must have — speed, availability, security, usability — rather than a feature. From Non-Functional Requirements Analysis [BABOK].
- **Observation** — watching work as it really happens (job-shadowing) to learn how a process actually runs when it differs from how people describe it. [BABOK]
- **Persona** — a short, named sketch standing in for a group of users, capturing their goals, context and frustrations. Part of Stakeholder List, Map, or Personas [BABOK].
- **PESTLE** — an environmental scan of Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal and Environmental factors. [industry]
- **Predictive** — BABOK's term for a plan-first, waterfall-style approach, where scope is agreed up front (contrast adaptive).
- **Prioritisation** — deciding the order in which requirements are delivered. BABOK's umbrella technique is Prioritization [BABOK], applied through methods such as MoSCoW and WSJF.
- **Product owner** — the agile role accountable for a product's value and for the priority order of its backlog; one person with the final say. [industry]
- **Prototype** — a sketch, wireframe or mock-up built to draw out or confirm requirements by letting people react to something tangible. From Prototyping [BABOK].
- **RACI** — a chart naming who is Responsible, Accountable (only ever one), Consulted and Informed for each activity. The best-known form of Roles and Permissions Matrix [BABOK]. [industry]
- **Requirement** — a need a solution must meet. BABOK classifies requirements as business, stakeholder, solution (functional and non-functional) and transition.
- **Root cause** — the underlying reason a problem exists, as opposed to its symptoms. Found through Root Cause Analysis [BABOK], which groups 5 Whys, Fishbone and Pareto.
- **RTM / Traceability** — a Requirements Traceability Matrix links each requirement up to the need it serves and down to its design, build and tests. Supports the BABOK task Trace Requirements. [industry]
- **Scope creep** — the uncontrolled growth of scope as changes are accepted without assessment; the commonest way a sound business case quietly dies.
- **SIPOC** — a one-page scoping table — Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers — that frames a process before you detail it. [industry]
- **Solution** — a way of meeting a business need: a new or changed system, process or capability. What the solution must do or be is captured as solution requirements.
- **Stakeholder** — anyone affected by, or who can affect, the change or its outcome. Stakeholders are identified and analysed early.
- **State diagram** — shows the states a thing (such as an order) moves through and the allowed transitions between them. From State Modelling [BABOK].
- **Swimlane** — a process map split into lanes, one per role or system, so hand-offs show up as arrows crossing lanes. [industry]
- **SWOT** — a synthesis of internal Strengths and Weaknesses with external Opportunities and Threats, used to inform decisions. From SWOT Analysis [BABOK].
- **To-be** — how a process or system should work after the change; the gap from the as-is is your requirements.
- **Transition requirement** — a temporary capability needed only to move from the current state to the future one — migration, training — which falls away afterwards.
- **UAT** — User Acceptance Testing: real users confirm the built solution meets its agreed acceptance criteria before go-live. Built on Acceptance and Evaluation Criteria [BABOK]. [industry]
- **Use case** — a description of how an actor uses the solution to reach a goal, via a main success scenario and its extensions. From Use Cases and Scenarios [BABOK].
- **User story** — a short, user-centred statement of a need — *As a [role], I want [capability], so that [benefit]* — a placeholder for a conversation. From User Stories [BABOK].
- **Value stream mapping** — a Lean map of the end-to-end flow that separates value-adding time from wait time, exposing how much of the lead time is just waiting. [industry]
- **VMOST** — an alignment check that an organisation's Vision, Mission, Objectives, Strategy and Tactics line up (MOST drops the Vision line). [industry]
- **Wireframe** — a low-fidelity sketch of a screen, used to confirm requirements before build. A form of Prototyping [BABOK].
- **WSJF** — Weighted Shortest Job First: ranks work by cost of delay divided by job size (from the Scaled Agile Framework, SAFe). [industry]
